Why this instrument

    
Measuring positions without party labels

    
Most political surveys ask people which side they are on. This one asks where they stand, one issue at a time, with no statement marked as the party line. The respondent never sees a label, so there is nothing to perform.

    
The design borrows from psychometrics rather than polling. Each domain is a short scale, half the statements in it lean one way and half the other, and the order is shuffled so that a habit of agreeing cannot drive the score. The result is a profile across domains rather than a single sorting into two bins.

    
Only final scores are kept, with no name attached. Item-level answers belong to the respondent and never leave their device unless they choose to copy them.
